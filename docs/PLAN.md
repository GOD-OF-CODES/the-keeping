# Plan — "THE KEEPING": a photoreal browser horror game, built 100% from scratch

## Context

The user wants a hyper-realistic, first-person horror game that runs in the browser (three.js; Blender allowed).
It opens with a scripted night-drive cutscene: low on fuel, forced stop at a sinister house, knock + ring the bell,
the door opens by itself, and in the first room on the right a man is beheading a woman. You stand frozen on the
threshold, both of them look at you, and the woman chases you upstairs. Plot after that, obstacles and the
"escape theorem" are ours to design. Required:
- 3 graphics presets (Low / Medium / Max), chosen **before any texture or asset download**
- an automatic per-device recommendation
- an FPS toggle in settings

Workflow: plan first, then build.

**User decisions (confirmed)**
- **Desktop/laptop only:** keyboard + mouse, pointer lock. Phones get a "best on desktop" notice.
- **Everything from scratch, no downloads.** No third-party content of any kind. Every mesh, texture, material,
  animation, sound, font and UI graphic is generated by our own code: Blender Python scripts, runtime procedural
  TSL shaders and Web Audio synthesis.
  - npm: `three@0.186.1`, `vite` and `typescript` only. Nothing else.
  - The UI uses system fonts. In-world handwriting and painted text use our own procedural stroke font.
  - The game loads only its *own* generated files (meshes, baked lighting, character textures) from our server,
    and only after the preset is chosen.
- **Short & polished:** ~10–15 min, ONE linear escape route, ONE ending, fewer spaces with more detail each.
- **Voices: the ONE exception to "no third-party generation."** Character voices are generated with **ElevenLabs**.
  To stay "from scratch", every voice is *designed* from our own written description (Voice Design API; no stock
  or library voices) and every line is our own script.
  - **All three characters speak.** The driver is a **man, mid-30s**.
  - The user has **no ElevenLabs account yet**. Everything else is built first. Lines run as timed subtitles until
    `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` is added to `.env.local`; then one command (`npm run voices`) generates them.
  - Everything else (SFX, score, breath fallbacks) stays synthesized from scratch.
- **Permission mode:** the user wants **bypass permissions** for the build. I can't switch modes myself: the user
  picks it in the approval prompt or with Shift+Tab. If it isn't offered, restart with
  `claude --dangerously-skip-permissions --continue`. Guardrails I keep regardless:
  - work only inside the project dir;
  - no deletes outside it, no git push;
  - never print or commit the API key;
  - one Blender process at a time.
- **Weak machine → never more than 3 agents running at once** (build included), and one Blender process at a time.
  Reading: at most 3 *subagents*; I (the coordinator) stay idle while they run.

**Expectations to confirm at approval**
- **Story theme.** With creative liberty, the plot is about a husband who drowned his wife and made her a revenant.
  It covers domestic violence, murder, drowning and cinematic gore, and there is a content warning at boot.
  Veto it now if unwanted; the design would change before any build hours are spent.
- **"Photoreal" here.**
  - The environment, lighting, rain, flashlight and film-like post-processing aim to read as photographic.
  - The two humans are deliberately *glimpsed* (darkness, motion, hidden faces), never inspected. Code-built humans
    can't survive close inspection.
- **Duration.** The last 10-agent planning workflow took ~3 h at the 3-agent cap. The build will take many hours,
  likely across sessions, with a report at every milestone.
- **Browsers.** Chrome/Edge (WebGPU) is the intended experience, and Firefox 147+ also runs WebGPU. Safari 18.6 on
  this Mac uses the WebGL2 fallback, with Max degraded.
- **Voices need an ElevenLabs account.** Until the user adds a key, the game is complete but plays its lines as
  subtitles. A paid plan (Starter, $6/mo: commercial license, 30k credits, 3 voice slots) comfortably covers the
  ~60-line script. Free (10k credits, 2 slots, personal use only) works with care.

## 1. Game design — THE KEEPING (~12.5 min)

**The story in plain words (as the player lives it).**

*Arrival.* 11 October 1994, a storm, a lonely county road, the fuel needle below E, the next town 48 miles away. An
old sign reads STROUD'S GAS & FEED with GAS painted out; under it hangs a lantern-lit ROOMS board. The engine dies
at the gate, and the only lit house for miles stands on the rise. You knock: nothing. You ring the bell: it rings
somewhere to the right, a whetstone stops rasping, ropes creak, and the door swings open by itself.

*The first room on the right.* A masked man stands over a woman bent across a table, cleaver raised for a second
stroke. He stops, turns her face to you by the hair, and both look at you. "Go on, then." He lets a rope slip, the
front door slams and bolts, and she rises with her head lolling and chases you up the stairs.

*Upstairs.* Hiding in a wardrobe, you hear her lift her own head with a crack of bone and whisper "…Harlan?" at the
slats. She isn't looking for you. From his ledger, her unsent letter, the guest book of eleven crossed-out names,
and your own car pushed into a field of wrecks, you learn what happened:
- Harlan drowned his wife Ada in the well in 1976, the night before she was leaving him.
- She came back hunting his face. Every night he cuts off her head to hold her for one night, and he hides his face
  under a sack.
- He lures travelers with the ROOMS sign to feed her strangers' faces instead. You're the twelfth.

*The way out.* The last photo of his face is in her locket, sewn into the hem of her wedding dress in a room nailed
shut since 1976. You lure her away with his bell, pry the boards off under the thunder, cut the locket free, slip
down the hidden servants' stair, and take a dead guest's gas can. (One empty can is already chalked with your plate.)

*The ending.* In the dark hall you don't run. You hold the open locket up in your flashlight and let her come. She
looks. "…Harlan." She takes the locket and knocks on his door. In shadow on the wall she pulls off his sack, and one
stroke falls. The rope runs, the front door opens for you onto grey dawn, and you drive away as the lantern dies.

*The sting.* Weeks later another traveler rings the bell. The guest book's newest line reads HARLAN, crossed out. In
the first room on the right Ada sits in his rocking chair and turns a sacked head's eyeholes to the doorway, just as
he once turned hers. You freed her from her keeper, not from the house.

**Logline.** Out of gas on County Road 9 in the rain (11 Oct 1994), you ring the bell of the only lit house for
miles, under a lantern-lit sign that says ROOMS. The door opens by itself. In the first room on the right a man in a
feed-sack mask is beheading a woman, and both of them turn to look at you. She hunts you through the house until you
work out whose face she has really been looking for.

**Lore (short).**
- *Ada and Harlan.* In 1976 Harlan Stroud drowned his wife Ada in the rain cistern under the kitchen (the "well") the
  night before she was to leave him on the 6:10 bus. She came back: a revenant of the unmourned drowned. She hunts
  one face, his, but the water clouded her eyes, so she must bring her face close to a face to know it.
- *The nightly ritual.* Every night he beheads her ("take the head, give it back to the water"), which holds her for
  one night. Six thousand pencil tallies on the parlor wall count those nights.
- *How he hides.* He hides his face: a burlap sack, crepe over every mirror, his face knifed out of every photo.
- *The lure.* He learned a stranger's face buys weeks of quiet. So he painted out GAS, hung a ROOMS board, wired every
  bell to ring in the parlor, and hung the front door on a rope.

**Twist.** Nothing in the opening was chance:
- The lantern was bait, the bell was his signal and the self-opening door was his rope.
- He stopped the second stroke on purpose and turned her face toward you by the hair.
- She was never hunting you. She is hunting him, and she can't see him.

**Way out.** The last image of his face is in her locket, sewn into the hem of her wedding dress, in a room nailed
shut since 1976. Hold his face in your light and let her look.

**The rope (one mechanism everywhere).** A counterweight shuts the front door and an iron drop-bolt locks it. One rope
runs over three cornice pulleys to a cleat in the parlor. Only a pull from the parlor opens it.

**Characters (staged to hide realism limits; all three voiced)**
- **Ada (the chaser).** Slight, barefoot, sodden ivory nightgown, grey-blue hands and feet. Long wet black hair covers
  her whole face; one clouded, iris-less eye is the most we ever show.
  - Her neck is half-severed, so the head lolls on a spring joint.
  - She moves in stop-motion (pose sampled at 8–12 fps while she moves smoothly through space).
  - She drips constantly and is never lit for more than 3 s.
  - **Voice:** a wet, broken whisper. Every time she lifts her head to look at a face she breathes **"…Harlan?"**,
    then a disappointed gurgle when it isn't him. This is a tell *and* the audible clue to P4 (she hunts his face).
    Also a near-sob at the dress ("…my ticket…") and the recognition "…Harlan." in the finale.
  - **Living voice:** her letter is read in her voice as it was in 1976 (warm, tired, determined), a deliberate
    contrast with the revenant.
- **Harlan (scripted only, never a chaser).** Big, stooped, sack mask stencilled STROUD FEED & SEED, flannel, rubber
  apron, boots, hog cleaver. His face is never rendered.
  - His only authored clip is the opening tableau (~15 s).
  - Everything else is static poses revealed by lightning, audio (rocking chair, bell), or shadows on a wall.
  - **Voice:** rare, slow, gravelly, muffled by the sack, 5–7 lines in total. Examples:
    - "Go on, then." when he lets the rope slip in C2
    - "She'll find you, friend. She always does." up through the floor grate
    - his ledger pages as voice-over
    - "Ada?… No. No, no—" at the door crack in C5
- **Player (the driver).** Man, mid-30s, tired, soft-spoken, American. Only gloved hands, coat sleeve and a steel
  two-cell flashlight are visible.
  - He talks and reacts, sparingly:
    - in the car: "Come on… don't do this to me." and "They'll have to put me up for the night."
    - at the door: "Hello? …Sorry to bother you this late — my car ran out of gas."
    - in hides: "Don't breathe. Don't breathe."
    - at the window: "That's… that's my car."
    - at the can: "RVX… that's my plate."
    - raising the locket: "Look. It's him. It's him you want."
  - Breathing, gasps and panting are voiced takes (synthesized fallbacks until the voices exist).
- **The next traveler (sting only).** A different voice (woman, 40s) repeats the opening line at the door, shot for
  shot.

**Chaser AI (deterministic, authored node graph, no navmesh)**
- **States:** SCRIPTED, VIGIL, PATROL, LISTEN, INVESTIGATE, LOOK, CHASE, SEARCH, LURED, FINALE, CATCH.
- **Priority:** FINALE > CATCH > SCRIPTED > CHASE > LOOK > newest(LURED | INVESTIGATE) > SEARCH > PATROL > VIGIL.
- **Speeds (m/s):**
  - Player: walk 1.6, run 3.6 (no stamina meter, but you pant), crouch 0.9.
  - Ada: patrol 0.9, investigate 1.4, chase 3.2 (in bursts).
- **Hearing** (noise radius at the source, in metres):

  | Crouch | Runner | Bare boards | Creaker | Run | Panting (4 s) | Door slow / fast | Pry | Gasp |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1.5 | 2 | 4 | 7 | 10 | 2.5 | 3 / 8 | 14 | 3 |

  - Attenuation happens on the **room graph**: each closed door ×0.5, each floor ×0.4, except through the open
    stairwell.
  - Thunder rolls (2.5 s, starting ~1.5 s after each flash) mask all player sound.
- **Light and sight:**
  - A beam touching her forces a fast LOOK; a beam within 6 m of her makes her INVESTIGATE.
  - She sees only with her head lifted (LOOK/CHASE): a 60° cone, 14 m if you are lit, 5 m if dark, 2 m if dark
    and crouched.
  - Lightning never counts as light. Standing within 1.5 m of a fixed flame makes you lit.
- **LOOK:** hand to her jaw, bone crack, 1.2 s wind-up (0.6 s if your beam touched her), 3 s of sight.
- **Hides:** safe unless she saw you within 2 s of entering, or you are audible within 2 m. Hold breath up to 7 s.
- **Locket rule:** an open, lit locket within 4 m inside her cone outranks your face and triggers FINALE, even mid-chase.
- **Tells:**
  - Drip: its rate follows her speed; drip stops = she is listening.
  - Bone crack = she will see in 1.2 s. Throat gurgle = within ~5 m.
  - A whispered "…Harlan?" as she looks. It doubles as the clue that she hunts *his* face.
  - Wet footprints that dry in ~60 s. Flames lean toward her.
  - Ceiling drips under her when she is on the floor above.
  - Nails on plaster = searching. The chase score with heartbeat.
  - Optional captions and icons for every tell.

**Escape theorem (deducible, linear)**
- Premises:
  - P1: the front door is the only exit (sashes nailed, back door bricked).
  - P2: it opens only by a pull from the parlor.
  - P3: Harlan holds the rope and means you for her, which gives **L1: only Ada can make him let go**.
  - P4: she hunts his face.
  - P5: seeing it, she will know him.
  - P6: his last photo is in her locket, sewn in the dress hem.
  - P7: she sees only when her head is lifted, and only what is lit.
  - Together these give **L2: put his face in your light and let her come close**.
- Route: survive → hammer + ledger → bell lure → pry 3 boards in thunder → letter + hem → locket → servants' stair →
  full can → raise the lit locket in the hall.

**Fail / checkpoints / assists**
- **Death:** a 3 s non-gory cutaway (wet hand, hair, eye glint, drowning rush), then an instant reload. Only your
  position is lost.
- **Checkpoints:** CP1 gate, CP2 hall, CP3 after the first hide, CP4 hammer/ledger, CP5 each board, CP6 locket,
  CP7 can, CP8 finale.
- **Grace after a death:** she restarts at the anchor farthest from you, PATROL only, with hearing -30% for 20 s; the
  lure resets to 60 s.
- **Assists:**
  - After 2 deaths in a section: she is slower, her LOOK wind-up is longer and noise radii are smaller.
  - After 3 deaths: distraction noises.
  - After 2 failed pries: longer thunder rolls.
  - Finale prompt after 1 failure; after 2, she waits.
  - Stuck for 90 s: lightning highlights the next interactable.
- Cutscenes are skippable after the first viewing.
- **Accessibility:** reduced-flash mode (flashes ≤ 3 Hz), captions, and a content warning at boot (domestic
  violence, murder, drowning, gore).

**Content budget:**
- 21 prop families, mostly parametric generators: house kit, door/window/stair generators, slatted-cabinet generator,
  furniture family, sedan + wreck variants, bells/rope rig, documents, and FX.
- 28 character/player animations.
- 8 cutscenes.
- 15 sound groups, all synthesized.
- ~60 voiced lines and vocal takes (ElevenLabs, §2.9).

The full lists are in `docs/design.json`.

## 2. Technical architecture

### 2.1 Stack & principles
- `three@0.186.1` **WebGPURenderer** (`three/webgpu`, TSL) for every preset. It falls back automatically to its
  WebGL2 backend (Safari 18.6 here, and Safari 26 on Sequoia). Low and Medium must be fully WebGL2-safe; no
  must-have feature may use compute or StorageTexture.
- TypeScript + Vite. Pure-logic tests run with Node's built-in runner:
  `node --experimental-strip-types --test tests/`. No test framework.
  - Constraints this implies for all code under test: explicit `.ts` import extensions; no `enum`, parameter
    properties or `namespace` (use `as const` objects); tsconfig sets `allowImportingTsExtensions` + `noEmit`.
    Vite bundles the same files.
- **Single sources of truth**, read by both the Blender Python scripts and the runtime:
  - `src/shared/level-layout.json`: rooms, dimensions, openings, doors and hinges, windows, stairs, hides, AI nodes,
    room-graph edges with attenuation, lights, material ids, collision proxies.
  - `src/shared/material-spec.json`: per-material generator params plus the average albedo and roughness used for
    Cycles bounce colour.
- **Photometric convention:**
  - Runtime point/spot intensity = Blender W / (4π), with no 683 factor.
  - `lightMapIntensity` = π, pending calibration smoke test S3.
  - One exposure for baked and realtime light; AgX tone mapping.

### 2.3 Boot, recommendation, settings (nothing downloads before the choice)
1. `index.html` and a tiny boot chunk (target ≤ 25 KB gz): system-font UI, content warning, "Detecting your device…".
   There is no static import chain into three or the game. `scripts/verify-boot.mjs` asserts the built `index.html`
   has no `modulepreload` for those chunks.
2. `detect()` gathers:
   - WebGL2 with `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat` (retry without it), plus `UNMASKED_RENDERER_WEBGL`. Chrome gives the
     full ANGLE string, e.g. "Apple M1"; Safari says "Apple GPU"; Firefox gives buckets.
   - `navigator.gpu.requestAdapter({powerPreference:'high-performance'})`, raced against a 1.5 s timeout, for
     `adapter.info.vendor`/`architecture`.
   - `deviceMemory` (lower-bound only), `hardwareConcurrency`, `(pointer: coarse)`, screen × DPR.
3. A ~1.5 s zero-asset micro-benchmark: a raw WebGL2 ALU-heavy fragment shader into a 512² FBO, synced by
   `readPixels`, with batch doubling, warm-up discard and a median.
   - **Fix the bug the critic found:** record a sample when the batch hits its cap and yield, otherwise fast GPUs
     return NaN.
   - Score = 100 · raw / REF[browser]. REF is calibrated on this M1 in Chrome (and in Safari).
4. `recommend()` makes the tier decision. Resolution never changes the tier; it only feeds the preset's scale.
   - Base M1–M4 by name → **Medium**.
   - Apple Pro/Max/Ultra, RTX xx60+ (30-series on), RTX 2070+, RX 6700+ → **Max**.
   - Iris Xe / UHD / Vega APU → **Low**, promoted to Medium only if score ≥ 120.
   - Masked or unknown → decided by score (< 55 Low, ≥ 175 Max, else Medium).
   - Software renderer, a failed performance-caveat check, or a touch-only device → **Low**.
   - The UI shows the reason, e.g. "Recommended: MEDIUM — Apple M1 GPU, 8 CPU threads, 1440×900 @2×, benchmark 100".
5. **The settings card:**
   - Low/Medium/Max, each with a spec summary and approximate download size.
   - **Show FPS counter** (toggle) and **Frame-rate cap** (30 / 60 / Uncapped, with divisor mode for 120/144 Hz).
   - Reduced flash.
   - Start.
   - Choices persist in `localStorage` (wrapped in try/catch).
6. **Start** is the user gesture that makes the AudioContext. It then calls `WEBGL_lose_context` on the benchmark
   context and runs `import('../game/main')`. The game then:
   - creates a **fresh canvas**, then the renderer: WebGPU if an adapter was found, else
     `forceWebGL:true` + our own `webgl2` context with `powerPreference:'high-performance'`;
   - fetches the tier manifest and assets;
   - GPU-generates textures;
   - pre-renders the audio banks;
   - bakes the LightProbeGrid;
   - runs `compileAsync` plus 2 warm-up frames of the pipeline;
   - shows "Click to turn the key", which also grabs pointer lock and starts C1.
7. **In-game pause menu (Esc):**
   - the same FPS toggle and cap
   - mouse sensitivity, invert Y, FOV
   - volume sliders, captions, reduced flash
   - preset change (saved, "applies after reload"; the renderer, MSAA and pipeline are fixed at creation)
   - "re-run device test"

**FPS counter:** our own meter showing a 1 s rolling average, 1% low and p99, updated 4×/s. The cap uses the
"due-deadline" algorithm with half-vsync tolerance and stall resync. `THREE.Timer` is fed from our loop;
`THREE.Clock` is deprecated.

### 2.5 Lighting & materials (where the photorealism comes from)

**Baked GI.** Blender Cycles (Metal GPU):
- Bake type `DIFFUSE` with pass_filter {DIRECT, INDIRECT} and no COLOR, i.e. irradiance.
- Bake into float images through UV2 (`uv_layer='Lightmap'`, exported as TEXCOORD_1 → `uv1`).
- Denoise afterwards with Blender's bundled **OIDN 2.5 via ctypes** (RT filter, `hdr=True`); Cycles never denoises
  bakes.
- Dilate before denoising. Save as EXR half.
- Rules:
  - Apply **all modifiers on static meshes before UV2**, the bake and the export.
  - Join meshes per atlas; each object is its own ~0.26 s Metal session.
  - Bake one atlas per Blender process (8 GB RAM).
  - Set `cycles.samples` and `use_adaptive_sampling=False` explicitly.
- Estimated full bake: ~11–44 min at 64–128 spp for the house; the lightning-flash maps are a second, smaller bake.
- Lower tiers are downsampled from the Max bake. UV padding is sized for the smallest tier.
- **RAM scheduling (8 GB):** day-to-day dev bakes are 512–1024² at 16–32 spp. Full-resolution bakes run as a
  dedicated serial step at milestone ends, with **no Chrome/WebGPU session or heavy agent work running at the same
  time**. Vite dev can stay up; the browser is closed.

**Runtime lightmap material.** A `MeshStandardNodeMaterial` subclass overrides `setupLightMap()` and returns
`new IrradianceNode(decode(base) + decode(flash) * uLightning)`.
- Texture: `channel = 1`, `NoColorSpace`.
- The EXR v-flip is done in the decode, and proven with an asymmetric test pattern (S2).
- Lightmapped materials set `material.lightsNode = lights([flashlight, …])` so the probe grid doesn't double-count
  indirect light.

**Dynamic objects** (characters, doors, hands, props you carry):
- A `LightProbeGrid` (L2 spherical harmonics) is baked **at load** from the lightmapped scene, with
  `bounces: 0` and the flashlight and characters hidden.
- A second grid for the lightning flash is driven by the same uniform.
- Per-room probe volumes keep the bake cost bounded; it is measured in S5.
- **Fallback** if the runtime bake is too slow or fails on WebGL2: Blender bakes small cubemaps at a few points per
  room, projects them to L2 SH in Python, and ships them as JSON. The runtime blends 2–3 `LightProbe`s by character
  position. Our own code either way.

**Flashlight:**
- `SpotLight` with shadows and a procedural TSL cookie via `colorNode(lightCoord)`: the hotspot ring and lens
  imperfections.
- Battery flicker, and the beam trembles near her.
- Dust motes in the beam; a volumetric beam on Max.

**Lightning:**
- A sky-card flash plus a directional pulse, exposure kick, window emissive and probe pulse.
- An additive flash lightmap in U1–U3 on Max.
- Thunder follows 1.5–4 s later.

**Candles and lamps:**
- Baked base light, plus a small unshadowed dynamic point light for flicker.
- Procedural flame shader; flames lean toward Ada.

**Procedural tiling textures, generated on the GPU at load (zero download):**
- A `RenderTarget + QuadMesh` baker, sized per preset.
- Our own **periodic** TSL noise library (the `mx_*` noises have no period).
- Packed as albedo+roughness and normal+AO/height in UnsignedByte targets, with mipmaps and anisotropy.
- Materials: varnished and bare floorboards, runner rug, 3 damask wallpapers (damp/peel), damp plaster, chipped trim,
  clapboard siding with peeling paint, wet porch boards, shingles, brick, gravel, wet asphalt, mud, bark, rust,
  cast iron, enamel, grimy/rain glass, dust-sheet fabric, crepe, car paint and interior.
- Anti-tiling: world-space macro variation, dust on up-facing surfaces, and grime from the lightmap-AO mask.
- Decals: blood, silt, footprints (wet prints dry out), chalk plates.
- Each generator reads `material-spec.json`, as the Blender bake materials do, so bounce colour matches.

**Unique baked textures** (Blender procedural node materials → character or prop UV0 → WebP per tier):
- skin (pale grey-blue, veins)
- nightgown (silt, stains, wet)
- burlap sack (weave + STROUD stencil)
- rubber apron, flannel, gloves
- cleaver, locket photo, sedan dashboard

**Documents:** our own single-stroke handwriting font, drawn with ink wobble on a CanvasTexture at runtime (guest
book, ledger, letter, chalk, the wet HARLAN).

**Wet look:** rain streaks and droplets on glass, drips, puddle ripples, roughness modulation, ceiling beads.

### 2.6 Characters & animation (from scratch)

**Shared skeleton:**
- ~60 bones: spine ×3, neck ×2, head, jaw, simplified fingers.
- Plus runtime chains for hair and gown.
- A separate first-person arms rig.

**Mesh:**
1. A skeleton-derived vertex graph with anatomical per-vertex radii goes through a Skin modifier and Subdivision.
2. Displacement modifiers driven by procedural textures shape muscles and bone landmarks, plus bmesh detailing.
3. **Apply the modifiers**, then weight with `parent_set(type='ARMATURE_AUTO')`. Bone-heat weighting was verified
   headless.
4. Shape keys come after applying: jaw, gurgle, head hang. Export with `export_apply=False`.

**Hair:** generated directly as bmesh card ribbons along guide splines: wet clumps plastered over the face, gravity.
- The alpha strand texture is baked.
- Runtime: alpha-hash (resolved by TAAU); alpha-test on Low.
- A verlet chain drives the hair bones. (Hair CURVES objects cannot export to glTF.)

**Clothes:**
- The nightgown and Harlan's sack are cloth-simmed on a rest pose by stepping frames (verified headless).
- Then applied, and bound by Data Transfer weights.
- Runtime spring chains give the gown and sack secondary motion.

**Eyes:** eyeball + cornea + procedural clouded iris (Ada's one-eye close-ups).

**Clips:**
- Authored by script with the **5.x layered Action API** (`slots → layers → strips → channelbag(slot, ensure=True)
  → fcurves.new`); the legacy `action.fcurves` is gone.
- A parametric gait generator (stutter-walk, lurch-run, stair climb) plus keypose tables (opening tableau, LOOK,
  vigil scrape, hide check, catch, dress, finale, sting).
- IK is baked with `nla.bake(visual_keying=True, clear_constraints=True)`.
- One clip per NLA track, exported with `export_animation_mode='ACTIONS'`.
- Also: `scene.render.fps=30`, in-place locomotion, stray actions deleted, `export_extras=True`.
- **One GLB per character** (ada.glb, harlan.glb, arms.glb): the exporter's single-armature action gathering assumes
  exactly one armature per export.

**Runtime:**
- `AnimationMixer` crossfades.
- Ada's **stop-motion**: the mixer time advances in 8–12 fps steps with random holds, while root motion, springs and
  the camera run at full rate.
- Neck spring (head loll), look-at, and `CCDIKSolver` for banister, door and locket contacts (`Vector3` limits).
- Harlan's non-opening appearances are static poses.

### 2.7 Gameplay systems

### 2.8 Audio (100% synthesized, Web Audio)
- **Engine:** the AudioContext is unlocked on Start.
  - Buses: ambience, weather, SFX, creature, player, score, UI. Master → compressor/limiter.
  - Pauses on `visibilitychange` and pointer-lock loss.
- **Banks:** pre-rendered in an `OfflineAudioContext` during loading, with seeded variations.
  - drips, bone crack, gurgle, nails on plaster, wood scrape, footsteps per surface, creakers, door creak
    (stick-slip), slam/bolt, knocks, doorbell and spring bell (modal), rope through pulleys
  - whetstone, chop (impact + wet + crack), engine sputter/stall/crank/catch, fuel chime, wipers
  - pry screech, shears, locket click, paper, can scrape, hatch thump
  - breath (calm / strained / held / gasp), heartbeat, score stingers
- **Live layers:** rain by surface (roof, glass, porch, car), wind, gutters, thunder (crack + roll locked to each
  flash), score drone and chase cluster.
- **Spatial:** HRTF `PannerNode` for every Ada tell.
  - Occlusion and gain use the **same room-graph attenuation as her hearing**: what she hears, you hear.
  - Each room has a procedural convolution reverb generated from its size and damping.
- **Voices come from §2.9.** Until they exist, the player's breathing, gasps and panting and Ada's gurgles use
  synthesized fallbacks, which are built anyway.

### 2.9 Voices (ElevenLabs, designed from scratch)

**Cast.** Each voice is designed from our own description (`POST /v1/text-to-voice/design`, model `eleven_ttv_v3`,
fixed `seed`). We get 3 previews per character, the user auditions and picks one (default: preview 1), and it is
saved with `POST /v1/text-to-voice` (`voice_name`, `voice_description`, `generated_voice_id`) to get a `voice_id`.
- **Driver:** man, mid-30s, American, tired and hoarse from a night drive, soft-spoken, close-mic'd; frightened but
  holding it together.
- **Harlan:** old rural American man, 60s, deep gravelly monotone, slow, heavy breathing, quiet menace; sounds as if
  speaking through a burlap sack.
- **Ada (1976):** woman, late 20s, rural American, warm but exhausted, quietly determined. The revenant is the *same
  voice* recorded with `[whispers]`-style delivery and processed at runtime (below).
- **Next traveler (sting):** woman, 40s, polite, rain-soaked, nervous.

**Script.** `src/shared/voice-script.json` holds ~60 lines (~4–6 k characters). Fields per line:
- `id`, `speaker`
- `text` with audio tags (e.g. `[whispers]`, `[exhales]`, `[crying]`)
- `subtitle` (tag-free)
- `trigger` (beat/event)
- `spatial` (in-head / positional / through-floor)
- `variants`, and `previous_text`/`next_text` for continuity

It includes vocal banks as short takes: driver breaths, gasps, panting and whimpers; Ada's "…Harlan?" ×5 variants,
wet exhales and sob.

**Generator.** `scripts/voices.mjs` (Node 22 `fetch`; no SDK dependency) handles one character at a time:
1. `design`, then `create`, then speak every line via
   `POST /v1/text-to-speech/{voice_id}/with-timestamps?output_format=mp3_44100_128`.
   - Model `eleven_v3`, which has proven audio tags. `eleven_v4` (released 2026-09-28) gets an A/B on 3 lines, and
     we pick by ear. The model id is configurable.
2. Decode `audio_base64`, loudness-normalise with ffmpeg (`loudnorm`, dialogue ≈ −16 LUFS), and write
   `public/assets/voice/<id>.mp3` plus `<id>.json` (word timings from `alignment`) for subtitles.
3. Cache by hash(text, voice, model, settings, seed), so re-runs cost zero credits.
   - `--dry-run` validates the script and prints a credit estimate without any network call.
   - On a free plan (2 voice slots), the voice can be deleted after its lines are rendered.

**Key safety.**
- `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` lives only in `.env.local` (git-ignored). It is read only by the Node script, never uses a
  `VITE_` prefix (so Vite can't bundle it), and is never printed.
- The verify step greps `dist/` for it.

**Runtime.**
- Voices sit on their own bus and load *after* preset selection like every asset (~3–4 MB, same on all tiers).
- Subtitles are word-timed from the alignment. Without audio, lines play as subtitles timed at ~2.7 words/s, so the
  game is fully playable before any key exists.
- Driver: in-head (non-spatial), whisper EQ in hides.
- Harlan: positional + room-graph occlusion (through floors = heavy low-pass) + a "sack" band-pass.
- Ada: HRTF-positional plus our own revenant chain: slight pitch-down, 7–11 Hz amplitude wobble from filtered noise
  (the "wet gurgle"), WaveShaper grit, band resonances and the room's convolution reverb.

- Commercial release requires a paid ElevenLabs plan (Starter or higher). The free plan is personal use only.

## 5. Key risks → mitigations
- **Human realism.**
  - Mitigations: faces never shown, stop-motion, backlight, grain, depth of field, lit shots under 3 s.
  - Wet hair and stop-motion get an A/B prototype in the first M1 week.
- **WebGL2 fallback is untested.** Test early (S5); Low and Medium are designed WebGL2-safe; Max degrades gracefully.
- **Bake time and 8 GB RAM.** One atlas per process, joined meshes, 64–128 spp + OIDN, input-hash caching,
  lower tiers downsampled.
- **Photometric mismatch (baked vs realtime).** S3 calibration and a single exposure.
- **Performance on M1 at Medium.** TAAU at 0.75, one shadow caster, room culling, dynamic resolution, shader warm-up.
- **Synthesized-audio realism.** Prototype the tells first; keep screams absent, distant or reverberant; layer sounds.
