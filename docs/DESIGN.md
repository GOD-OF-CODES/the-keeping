# THE KEEPING — Game Design Document

> Source: design judge panel (narrative / systems / production designs → horror-craft + buildability judges → synthesis). Raw data: `docs/design.json`. Voice additions and tech adjustments are in the approved plan (see `docs/PLAN.md`).

**Logline.** Out of gas on a county road in the rain, you ring the bell of the only lit house for miles, under a lantern-lit sign that says ROOMS. The door opens by itself. In the first room on the right a man in a feed-sack mask is beheading a woman, and both of them turn to look at you. She hunts you through the house until you work out whose face she has really been looking for.

**Playtime:** ~12.5 min

## Lore

SETTING. County Road 9, emptied by the interstate. Tuesday 11 October 1994: no phone, a cassette deck, a fuel needle below E.

THE HOUSE. Stroud's Gas & Feed served the road until its pump ran dry in the early seventies. Harlan Stroud married Ada Lind, a seamstress from Carvel, in 1968. By 1976 the marriage was locks, bruises and a bell: when Harlan rang, Ada came. She saved sewing money in a biscuit tin, wrote to her sister Ruth, and bought a seat on the 6:10 bus for Tuesday 12 October. Harlan found the tin. On the night of the 11th he held her under in the rain cistern beneath the kitchen floor (the well) and padlocked the hatch. He told Carvel she had run off. Nobody looked for her, mourned her or buried her.

WHAT SHE IS. Whatever the water keeps comes back up. Three nights later Ada climbed out: a revenant, one of the unmourned drowned, who wants one thing, the face of the man who held her under. The water clouded her eyes, so she must bring her face close to a face to know it. Any face that is not his she drags down into the well, and each stranger buys weeks of quiet. She cannot get past iron nails. She still comes to the bell. She remembers her room and her dress. And she learns.

WHAT HE IS. Nothing supernatural: a man who murdered his wife and made himself her jailer. His mother's receipt book said to take the head and give it back to the water. That holds her one night; about six thousand pencil tallies on the parlor wall count those nights. He hides the only way that works: a feed sack over his head, crepe over every mirror, his face knifed out of every photograph. The locket he gave her, the one image he could not destroy, he believes went down the well with her. In 1977 a drifter kept her quiet a month, and Harlan learned he could buy rest. He painted out the GAS on his sign and hung a ROOMS board, lit with gasoline siphoned from the last guest's car. He wired every bell to ring in the parlor, hung the front door on a rope and nailed every sash. His guest book holds eleven names; nine cars rust in the east field. She has to take them herself: one he killed himself didn't hold her an hour. He tells his ledger he does it so she can't walk the road. That is also true.

THE ROPE (one mechanism, obeyed everywhere). A counterweight pulls the front door shut. An iron drop-bolt, boxed out of reach above the transom, locks it. One rope runs from the bolt over three cornice pulleys to a cleat in the parlor. A pull lifts the bolt and draws the door open. Cleated, it stays open. Released, the weight slams it and the bolt drops. Only a hand in the parlor can open it.

THE TWIST. Nothing in the opening was chance. The ROOMS lantern was bait. The bell you rang was his signal, and the door that opened by itself was his rope. The beheading was his nightly chore, eighteen years to the night, and he stopped the second stroke on purpose. Both of them looked at you because he turned her face to the doorway by the hair so she would see yours. Then he let the rope go, and the slam and the stairs herded you upward like the eleven before you. She was never hunting you. She is hunting him, and she can't see him.

THE WAY OUT. The last image of his face is in the locket, sewn into the hem of her wedding dress beside her bus ticket in a room nailed shut since 1976. Hold his face up in your light and let her look.

THE STING. She takes him, pulls his rope and lets you go. But she learns: whoever holds the rope keeps the house.

## Characters

### The Traveler
*Player character: first person, silent, unnamed, driving County Road 9 on 11 October 1994.*

**Appearance.** Never seen. The player only sees leather driving gloves, a rain-dark coat sleeve and a steel two-cell flashlight. A tired late-80s sedan, plate RVX-318, is shown clearly in the opening because it pays off on a chalked can and in the field.

**Behavior.** No voice. One subtitle thought in the opening: 'Forty-eight miles to anything. They'll have to put me up for the night.' Camera and hands carry the emotion: the lock-up at the threshold, the beam trembling when she is near, held breath and heartbeat in hides, panting after sprints.

### Ada Stroud (the woman)
*The single AI chaser: a revenant, one of the unmourned drowned, and the story's true victim.*

**Appearance.** Slight and barefoot, in a sodden ivory nightgown stained grey-green with cistern silt. Grey-blue skin shows only at the hands and feet. Very long black hair is plastered wet over her whole face; one clouded, iris-less white eye is the most ever shown. She drips constantly. After the opening her neck is half-severed: the head hangs or lolls on a spring joint unless she lifts it in her hand. A pale line on her throat marks where a locket chain sat. She is seen in darkness, silhouette or through slats, and never lit for more than 3 s.

**Behavior.** Stop-motion stutter: her pose is sampled at 8-12 fps while she moves smoothly through space.
- Head hanging means she is blind; she hunts by sound and by the warmth of light.
- To see, she lifts her head in her hand with a bone crack, the key tell.
- She opens ordinary doors, slowly, but can't get past nails. Between hunts she stands at her own boarded door, forehead to the planks, scraping at the nail heads.
- She always goes to a ringing bell; in life, when he rang, she came.
- She goes close to every face she finds.
- She is human at the dress.
- She learns.

### Harlan Stroud (the man)
*Scripted presence only, never a chaser: husband, murderer, jailer and trapper.*

**Appearance.** Big, stooped, sixties. Burlap feed sack over his head (faded STROUD FEED & SEED stencil, uneven eyeholes, twine at the neck), flannel, suspenders, black rubber apron, rubber boots, gloves, hog cleaver. His face is never rendered; his unmasking is the shadow of a bare head on a wall.

**Behavior.** Slow; never runs, never attacks ('She has to take them herself'). His rocking chair creaks up through the floors like a metronome and stops when he moves. Appearances:
- the opening tableau, his only authored clip
- three static lightning poses: pushing your car, looking up at your window, watching from the foot of the stairs
- rocking under the floor grate, tilting the sack up at you
- the sack in the parlor door gap, then a pre-rendered shadow
- his head in a sack in the sting

His bell is heard, never seen. He watches every guest be taken.

### The next traveler
*Point of view for the final sting only.*

**Appearance.** Unseen: the same sedan interior re-trimmed (maroon, pine air freshener, fuel lamp blinking).

**Behavior.** Knocks, rings the bell and freezes on the threshold: the player's opening, shot for shot.

## Beats

### B01 — Empty (Car interior to County Road 9, at the gate, ~1 min, M1)
Opening cutscene C1 (about 55 s, in-engine, first person). Night, hard rain, fog. Wipers, radio static, the needle under E, the low-fuel chime. NEXT SERVICES 48 MI slides past. Out of the dark comes an old STROUD'S GAS & FEED sign with GAS painted out; a hand-lettered ROOMS board and a tin VACANCY plate creak under a lantern. The engine coughs and dies, and the car rolls to a stop at the gate. Lightning shows a peeling clapboard house on a rise: nailed shutters, a dead pump, one window right of the door leaking candlelight. Subtitle: 'Forty-eight miles to anything. They'll have to put me up for the night.' The camera rises past the taillights on plate RVX-318 as you step into the rain. No phone, no other light for miles: the only choice is to ask to stay the night. Tension 2 to 4.

**Player goal.** Watch; step out and go to the house.

**Set-piece.** The car dies under the lantern-lit ROOMS sign; lightning reveals the house.

### B02 — Stroud's (Gate, gravel drive, porch, ~1 min, M1)
First control; the flashlight (F) is needed at once. Walk up the drive past the dead pump (dial frozen at 1976 prices): no gas here either. Nail heads glint in every sash. Through the parlor shutters to the right of the door, a shadow rises and falls to the rasp of a whetstone. Knock: nothing, and the rasp goes on. Pull the bell knob: a bell rings inside, off to the RIGHT, and the rasp stops. A long beat. Inside, overhead, a rope runs through pulleys, the latch clacks, and the front door swings open by itself onto a dark hall. Without text, this teaches that the doorbell rings in the parlor and that the door is worked from in there. Tension 4 to 6.

**Player goal.** Find someone who will let you stay: knock, then ring the bell.

**Set-piece.** The door that opens by itself.

### B03 — The First Room on the Right (Entrance hall to the parlor threshold, ~0.75 min, M1)
Inside: a candle beside the open guest book (optional read: eleven names ruled through, tonight's date with the name blank). A crepe-covered mirror, and a wedding portrait with the groom's face knifed out. Look up and you see the rope running from a bolt box above the door into the parlor. The first door on the right stands ajar and candlelit, and from it comes one wet chop.

At the threshold the controls lock and the player freezes (C2, about 30 s). A candle on the near end of a long table throws the scene huge across a far wall covered in pencil tallies. A man in a feed-sack mask and rubber apron stands over a woman bent face-down across the table on a rubber sheet; spatter glistens, and her wet black hair hangs to the floor. His second stroke is raised, and stops. He takes a fistful of her hair and turns her face to the doorway. One clouded eye opens behind the hair. The sack's eyeholes turn to you. Both of them look at you for three heartbeats.

He lets the rope slip from its cleat. It runs over your head, and behind you the front door slams and the bolt drops. She slides off the table and rises, her head lolling on the half-cut neck; behind her the parlor door swings shut and a key turns. Control returns mid-lurch. Tension 6 to 10.

**Player goal.** Frozen at first; then run.

**Set-piece.** The beheading as shadow-play on the tally wall; the double look; the door slam.

### B04 — Up (Hall, main stair, upper hallway, ~0.4 min, M1)
Scripted chase with a guaranteed near-miss: she is held 2-4 m behind and catches you only if you stand still for more than 2 s. The front door won't budge, the parlor is shut, and the far end of the hall is a dead end (its passage door is bolted from the other side). Lightning shows the stair as the only way. She follows in stuttering bursts, one hand on the banister, head swinging. At the top the hallway doubles back along the balustrade, so you see her climbing through the balusters. A flash through the south window picks out an armoire standing ajar. Tension 10.

**Player goal.** Get away from her and hide.

**Set-piece.** Chase up the stairs with her visible through the balusters.

### B05 — Hold Your Breath (Upper hallway: the armoire, then her vigil, ~0.85 min, M1)
The first hide is taught by demonstration and cannot be failed. Through the slats you hear wet slaps on the stairs and the drip. At the top she stops, and the drip stops too: she is listening. She comes down the runner and stands at the slats. A gurgle; a crack of bone as her hand lifts her head; in a lightning flash the hair parts on one white eye. Prompt: hold breath (Space). She lowers her head, the drip resumes, and she drifts back to the door beside the stair top, boarded with three planks. She stands with her forehead against it, scraping at the nail heads. Now sneak out. She is about 5 m away, blind and listening: walking on the runner (2 m radius) or crouching (1.5 m) is safe, running (10 m) is not. Slip 1.5 m south into Harlan's bedroom. Checkpoint; the vertical slice ends here. Tension 10 to 5.

**Player goal.** Stay hidden while she looks; then sneak away from her vigil.

**Set-piece.** The eye at the slats; the woman scraping at her own nailed door.

### B06 — His Room (Harlan's bedroom and the upper hallway, ~1.75 min, M2)
A stealth loop you can read. She cycles from her door to the stair top (LOOK), down the runner, glances through Harlan's two doorways, stops at the south window (LOOK), and goes back. Her footprints glisten in the beam and dry out, and the drip gives her away through walls. Once, a thunder roll audibly swallows her footsteps.

Harlan's bedroom sits over the parlor, and its two doors make a loop. The room holds:
- the ledger (three short pages) beside a low lamp
- an embroidered bell pull whose wire runs toward the stair
- a wardrobe of strangers' coats (hide)
- a chair of spare sacks and twine where he makes his faces
- a crepe-covered mirror
- on the nailed window's sill, a claw hammer and a can of square nails
- a floor register looking straight down into the parlor: the tally wall, the table, and the rocking chair, empty but still rocking

Tension 5 to 6.

**Player goal.** Look for a way out; read the ledger; take the hammer.

**Set-piece.** The floor-grate view down into the parlor.

### B07 — He Looks Up (Harlan's bedroom: east window, ~0.5 min, M2)
Lifting the hammer triggers C3; you keep look control.
- Flash: nine dead cars in the weeds.
- Flash: the man in the sack pushing YOUR car (RVX-318) off the drive to join them.
- Flash: he has stopped and lifted his head to your window.
- Darkness.
- Flash: gone.

Below, the front door slams and the bolt drops, and the rocking resumes under your feet. The grate now shows him in the chair with the cleaver across his knees; if you peek, the sack tilts up at you. No help is coming; you are the next car. Tension 6 to 8 to 6.

**Player goal.** Understand there is no help and no car.

**Set-piece.** The field of cars and the man who looks up.

### B08 — Nailed Shut (Upper hallway: Ada's door beside the stair top, ~1.5 min, M2)
The one door you can't open is boarded, and it is the door she keeps standing at. Pull the bell by his bed: far below the parlor bell jangles, the drip recedes down the stairs, and through the grate you hear her scraping at the parlor door. The storm is at its peak, with a flash every 12 s or so. Pry each board inside a thunder roll: flash, count, rumble, pull. A pry outside the rumble is a 14 m screech that carries down the stairwell and brings her back. In one flash a figure stands at the foot of the stairs, sack tilted up, watching; in the next he is gone. If she returns, hide in the armoire. Boards you have pried stay pried. Each later pull holds her downstairs for less time (60, then 50, then 40 s): she learns. Tension 5 to 8.

**Player goal.** Open Ada's door without bringing her back.

**Set-piece.** Prying in the thunder; the watcher at the foot of the stairs.

### B09 — The Hem (Ada's sewing room, ~1.5 min, M2)
Untouched since 1976. Dust sheets over a chair, a trunk and a treadle-machine shape. By the window stands the wedding dress from the hall portrait, its sheet slid off one shoulder and the hem exposed. In the sewing basket: shears and an unsent letter. Cut the hem (hold) and a bundle drops: a 6:10 bus ticket for 12 October 1976 and a locket engraved 'A. from H. 1968'. Inside is a water-bloomed photo. Most of the image is gone, but not a young man's eyes and jaw: the only face of Harlan left in the world.

The drip returns on the landing. Hide in her wardrobe (C4). Through the slats she goes to the dress, her hand finds the cut hem, and she makes a long wet sound that is almost a sob. Her head lifts toward you: hold your breath, and the boards at your back give a little. She leaves. The wardrobe's back breathes cold air; push through to the servants' stair. Tension 6 to 9 to 4.

**Player goal.** Find what Harlan fears; survive her visit.

**Set-piece.** Ada and her wedding dress, seen from the hide.

### B10 — What the Water Keeps (Servants' stair to the kitchen, ~1 min, M2)
A narrow stair choked with cobwebs, with one turn; the slosh below grows louder. In the kitchen the well's hatch is padlocked on the OUTSIDE and splintered upward from beneath; water seeps up, and something thumps once against the lid. Water beads on the ceiling and drips as she crosses the floor above. A row of jerry cans is chalked with licence plates, full of the dead guests' gasoline, and at the end sits a fresh, empty can already chalked RVX-318. Take a full one: your tank is empty. It grates on the floor. Through the shared wall the rocking stops, and the parlor bell starts ringing and doesn't stop. All night the bell pulled her away from you; now you are on the parlor's floor, and the drip starts down the main stair toward you. Slide the back-passage bolt. Tension 6 to 8.

**Player goal.** Learn the truth; take fuel; head for the front door.

**Set-piece.** The thump under the hatch; your plate on the empty can.

### B11 — Let Her Look (Back passage, entrance hall, parlor threshold, ~1.25 min, M2)
She is coming down the stair. Duck into the under-stair closet and she passes over your head, dripping through the treads onto your gloves. The front door is still bolted by the rope. Everything says run. Instead: light on, raise the open locket into the beam in front of your face (RMB), and let her come. She stops at arm's length. The crack; her hand lifts her head; one eye. The locket is sharp in the beam, her face soft behind it. Her grey fingers close over it, and the chain slides from your glove. The hall goes silent, and a beat later the bell stops.

She knocks on the parlor door three times, slowly: your knock. The door opens a crack as he checks, showing candlelight and the sack's eyeholes. Her hand is in the gap before he can shut it. You are walked to the threshold, framed exactly as in the opening. He backs away, raising the cleaver by reflex; she walks into it; the candle dies. Lightning: her shadow pulls the sack from his head. Next flash: her shadow lifts his cleaver. Black. One stroke. The bell rings once. Then the rope zips through the pulleys overhead, and behind you the front door swings open onto grey mist. The parlor door closes. Tension 8 to 10 to 2.

**Player goal.** Show her his face.

**Set-piece.** The locket look; the unmasking in shadow; the door that opens for you.

### B12 — Blue Hour (Porch, drive, near end of the wreck row, road, ~0.5 min, M2)
Release (C6, semi-interactive). The rain has stopped; the eaves drip; mist lies in the field with the wrecks as grey shapes. Your car sits at the near end of the row. Pour the can (hold) and turn the key: two coughs, then it catches, and the radio comes back mid-song. Roll past the pump; the ROOMS lantern gutters out as you pass. In the mirror the house sinks into the mist. Tension 2 to 1.

**Player goal.** Leave.

**Set-piece.** The engine catches.

### B13 — The Keeping (Road, hall, parlor threshold (another traveler), ~0.5 min, M2)
Sting C7, reusing the opening's camera paths. Weeks later, in the rain, a different dashboard with the fuel lamp blinking. The ROOMS lantern is lit again. A knock; the bell rings off to the right; the door opens by itself. In the guest book, the line that waited for your name reads HARLAN in wet, clumsy capitals, ruled through, with a blank line under it. From the opening's exact threshold and lens: candlelight, the tally wall with a fresh column of clumsy strokes, and Ada in his rocking chair with a round, heavy feed sack tied at the neck in her lap. The rocking stops. She lifts her own head in one hand; with the other she raises the sack by its knot and turns its eyeholes to the doorway, as he once turned hers. Both of them look at you. Black. One bell. THE KEEPING.

**Player goal.** None. Watch.

**Set-piece.** The mirrored tableau.

## Chaser AI

**States**
- SCRIPTED: cutscenes and set pieces. The opening rise; the B04 chase (held 2-4 m behind, catch only if you stand still for more than 2 s); the B05 slats look; the B09 dress visit.
- VIGIL: anchor between hunts. Upstairs at her own boarded door (beside the dress after B09), forehead to the planks, scraping the nail heads for 15-30 s; blind, hearing normal.
- PATROL: 0.9 m/s stutter-walk on an authored node loop, head hanging, with LOOK points at the stair top, the south window and the doorways. Upper loop: her door, stair top, runner, Harlan's two doors, south window. Ground loop (finale only): stair foot, parlor door, back passage, kitchen.
- LISTEN: freezes about 1 s, head tilted toward a noise; the drip stops.
- INVESTIGATE: walks at 1.4 m/s to a noise or light, then LOOKs.
- LOOK: her hand lifts her head with a bone crack. 1.2 s wind-up (0.6 s if your beam touched her), 3 s of sight, then the head lowers.
- CHASE: jerky 3.2 m/s bursts with her head held up in one hand. She follows running noise when sight breaks, and switches to SEARCH after 4 s with neither.
- SEARCH: up to two LOOKs, nails dragged on plaster; in Harlan's room she looks under the bed (head flops upside down, side-on). Then 4-6 s listening at the nearest hide.
- LURED: any bell, except during CHASE. She scrapes at the parlor door for 60 s, then 50, then 40 s (floor, reset on death), then climbs back up audibly.
- FINALE: triggered whenever she sights the open, lit locket within 4 m, in LOOK, in CHASE or during the dress visit. She stops dead, comes to arm's length, looks, takes it, and carries it to the parlor door; C5 plays when you are in the hall.
- CATCH: within 1 m in CHASE, bumping into her, or when she opens an occupied hide. Grab, then a 3 s death cutaway.

**Senses.** SOUND (primary, always on). Every action has a noise radius at its source:
- crouch 1.5 m
- walk on the runner 2 m; walk on bare boards 4 m; the two marked creakers 7 m
- run 10 m, then post-sprint panting 2.5 m for 4 s
- door slow 3 m / fast 8 m
- pry 14 m
- gasp in a hide 3 m

Attenuation uses the authored room graph, not raycasts: each closed door x0.5, each floor x0.4, except through the open stairwell. During a thunder roll (2.5 s, starting about 1.5 s after each flash) she hears nothing.

LIGHT (always on; she feels it as warmth). A beam touching her body forces an instant fast LOOK toward it. A beam playing within 6 m of her draws her to INVESTIGATE. Fixed flames never lure her, but standing within 1.5 m of one makes you lit.

SIGHT (only while her head is lifted, during LOOK and CHASE). 60-degree cone: 14 m if you are lit, 5 m if you are dark, 2 m if you are dark and crouched. Lightning never counts as light. Being seen starts a CHASE; being seen entering a hide means that hide is found. The open, lit locket within 4 m outranks everything else she sees.

**Rules.** 1) No cheating: she knows where you are only through a sense event.
2) Head hanging means blind, always. She sees only in LOOK or CHASE, and only what is lit.
3) Priority: FINALE > CATCH > SCRIPTED > CHASE > LOOK > newest of LURED/INVESTIGATE > SEARCH > PATROL > VIGIL. She acts on the newest stimulus that reaches her. A bell reaches her anywhere in the house; a noise reaches her only within its attenuated radius. A bell never breaks a CHASE: a pull during a chase is remembered, and she goes down to the parlor when the chase ends without a catch. Outside a chase, a pull overrides everything else, even a look already winding up.
4) She opens ordinary doors, slowly and audibly. She cannot get past nails (her boarded door until it is pried, every nailed sash). She never opens the locked parlor door herself and never uses the servants' stair.
5) Hides (armoire, two wardrobes, under-stair closet) are safe unless she saw you within 2 s of entering or you are audible within 2 m. At the slats, hold your breath (Space, up to 7 s). A gasp makes her tear the door open.
6) To lose her: break line of sight, then stop running; after 4 s she SEARCHes.
7) Each successive bell pull holds her for less time (60/50/40 s, reset on death). She learns.
8) Thunder rolls mask all player sound.
9) Speeds: you walk 1.6, run 3.6 (no stamina, but you pant), crouch 0.9 m/s. She patrols at 0.9, investigates at 1.4, chases at 3.2. Straight lines gain ground but never lose her; corners, doors, Harlan's two-door loop and the servants' stair do.
10) Locket rule: whenever she is sighted and the open, lit locket is within 4 m in her cone, it outranks your face and she enters FINALE.
11) There is only one of her, never relocated where you can see; scripted moves happen behind doors or floors.
12) Harlan never attacks: he rings, watches and waits.

**Tells.** - Drip: constant, spatialized, audible through walls; its rate follows her speed.
- Drip stops: she is listening; freeze.
- Bone crack and her hand rising to her jaw: she will see in 1.2 s.
- Throat gurgle: she is within about 5 m.
- Wet footprints glisten in the beam and dry over about 60 s.
- Candle and lamp flames lean toward her.
- Water beads on the ceiling under wherever she stands on the floor above.
- Nails on plaster: searching. Scraping at wood: at her vigil or lured to the parlor.
- Parlor bell: she is going downstairs.
- Head hanging: blind. Head held in her hand: she sees.
- A string cluster and heartbeat while she chases, falling silent when she loses you.
- Optional captions/icons for every tell.

**Counterplay.** - Listen before you move. Walk the runner, crouch on bare boards, avoid the creakers.
- Flashlight off near her; never put the beam on her.
- When the drip stops, freeze. When you hear the crack, get out of her cone or into the dark.
- Break line of sight before hiding, and don't hide winded.
- Hold your breath at the slats.
- Do loud things (running, doors, prying) inside a thunder roll.
- Pull his bell to send her down, and spend the shrinking window well.
- Lose her through the bedroom's two doors or on the servants' stair.
- In the finale, reverse everything: light on, the open locket in the beam in front of your face, stand still, let her look.

## Obstacles & puzzles

### Every Bell Rings in His Room (the lure) — Harlan's bedroom bell pull, sending her to the parlor door
**Solution.** Pull the embroidered bell pull. The parlor bell rings, and she drops anything except a chase to go downstairs and scrape at the parlor door: 60 s, then 50, then 40 s (reset on death). Then she climbs back up, audibly. The upper floor is clear while she is down there.

**Clues.** In B02 the doorbell rang off to the right, in the parlor, and the whetstone stopped. The bell wire runs from the pull along the cornice toward the stair. Ledger p3: 'She always comes to the bell.' On first use you hear the drip recede down the stairs and scraping through the floor grate.

### Nailed Shut (pry in the thunder) — Ada's boarded door beside the stair top (her vigil spot)
**Solution.** Take the hammer. Pry the three boards one at a time, each within a thunder roll (each pry is a 2 s hold). During this beat the storm flashes about every 12 s, so three rolls fit inside one bell window. A pry outside a roll is a 14 m screech that brings her back: hide, ring again, and continue. Pried boards stay pried. After 2 failures the rumbles last longer.

**Clues.** Ledger p1: 'Nailed her room shut. She can't get past nails.' She keeps scraping at that door. Ledger p3: 'She can't hear a thing under thunder.' In B06 a roll swallowed her footsteps. The hammer sits beside a can of the same nails. The storm keeps a steady rhythm: flash, count, rumble.

### The Hem — Ada's sewing room
**Solution.** Read the letter in the sewing basket. Cut the dress's hem with her shears and take the locket (and the ticket). Survive her visit in the wardrobe by holding your breath when her head lifts. Then push through the wardrobe's loose back to the servants' stair.

**Clues.** Letter: 'the ticket's sewn in the hem of my wedding dress, with the locket.' The hall portrait shows her in that dress. Ledger p2: the picture 'went down the well with her'. That is why it matters, and why nobody looked. Ledger p1: 'I won't go in either.' In the wardrobe the boards give at your back, and cold air moans through them.

### Let Her Look (the finale) — Entrance hall, between the under-stair closet and the parlor door
**Solution.** Flashlight on. Raise the open locket (RMB) into the beam in front of your face and let her come. Whenever she has sight of the lit locket within 4 m (LOOK or a CHASE glance), she stops dead, looks and takes it. It works wherever she sees it, even in the dress scene; she then carries it down to his door, C5 plays when you are in the hall, and the drive waits on the fuel can. Running without it is not an instant fail: she chases, and the servants' stair lets you reset.

**Clues.** Ledger p2: 'If she ever sees it she'll know me.' The sack, the crepe on every mirror and the knifed photos all say his face is the weapon. Every encounter has taught you that she sees only what is lit, and only with her head lifted. His nonstop bell tells you this is the moment. A one-time prompt appears only after the first failure.

## Escape theorem

GOAL: leave alive through the front door and drive away.

PREMISES (each clued in the house):
P1 EXIT: The front door is the only way out. [Every sash is nailed and shuttered, with the same nails on his sill; the kitchen door is bricked; the front door won't rattle open.]
P2 ROPE: The door opens only when the rope is pulled from the parlor. [B02: pulleys run and the door swings open. C2: the rope runs overhead and the door slams and bolts. The rope runs along the cornice into the parlor; the bolt box is out of reach.]
P3 KEEPER: The rope is Harlan's, and he means you for her; neither of them can be fought. [The opening; guest book: eleven names ruled through, tonight's blank; nine dead cars; your car pushed in among them; your plate chalked on an empty can.]
=> L1: You leave only if Harlan stops holding the rope, and only Ada can make him.
P4 HUNT: She hunts one face, his, and he survives by hiding it. [She lifts her head to every face; the sack; the crepe; the knifed photos; ledger: 'She can't find me if she can't see me.']
P5 RECOGNITION: If she sees his face, she will know him. [Ledger: 'If she ever sees it she'll know me, and that's the end of me.']
P6 FACE: One picture of his face survives in her locket, and it is not in the well: it is sewn into the hem of her wedding dress. [Ledger p2 against the letter.]
P7 SIGHT: She sees only while her head is lifted, and only what is lit. [Every encounter.]
=> L2: Put his face in your light where she will look (the open locket, held in the beam in front of your face) and let her come close.

ROUTE (linear; each step needs the one before):
1. Survive the chase; slip past her vigil into Harlan's bedroom.
2. Take the hammer; read the ledger; find the bell pull.
3. Pull the bell: she goes down to the parlor door for about 60 s.
4. Pry her three boards, one per thunder roll.
5. Read the letter; cut the hem: the locket.
6. Survive her visit in the wardrobe; go through its back and down the servants' stair to the kitchen.
7. Take a full can (your tank is empty).
8. Unbolt the back passage. In the hall, as his bell calls her onto you, raise the lit locket and let her look.

THEREFORE: she knows him, takes the locket and knocks at his door. Harlan opens a crack to check, and she takes him. She holds the rope now; she pulls it, and the front door opens. Can, tank, road.

COROLLARY (the sting): whoever holds the rope keeps the house, and she learns. [Ledger: 'She works the latches now. She learns.']

## Fail states & checkpoints

Death is quick and not gory: a 3 s cutaway (a wet hand over the lens, hair filling the frame, one eye glint, black, a bubbling drowning rush), then an instant reload. Only position is lost. Items, pried boards, opened doors, read documents and the can all persist, and key items can't be dropped.

Checkpoints:
- CP1: at the gate after C1
- CP2: the hall as she rises (C2 replays in 3 s)
- CP3: after the first hide
- CP4: taking the hammer or ledger
- CP5: after each pried board
- CP6: taking the locket (the dress visit replays)
- CP7: taking the can
- CP8: the finale

Grace: after a death she restarts at the anchor farthest from you, PATROL-only, with hearing -30% for 20 s, and the bell lure resets to 60 s. The first hide can't be failed.

Assists:
- After 2 deaths in a section: she is 15% slower, her LOOK wind-up grows to 1.6 s, and noise radii shrink 20%.
- After 3: a shutter bangs far away to draw her off, or thunder comes on cue.
- After 2 failed pries: longer rumbles.
- Finale: running isn't an instant fail. After one finale death the scene restarts with the locket raised and a one-time prompt; after two, she waits at 4 m.

If you are stuck for 90 s, lightning picks out the next interactable. Cutscenes can be skipped after the first viewing. The game pauses when pointer lock is lost.

## Ending — The Keeping

The only ending, earned by the escape theorem. Ada sees Harlan's face in your light and takes back her locket. In shadow on the tally wall, she ends the man who has ended her every night for eighteen years. Then she pulls his rope: he opened the door to bring you in, and she opens it to let you go. Blue hour, dripping eaves, mist over the dead cars. A dead guest's gasoline goes into your tank, the engine catches, the ROOMS lantern gutters out, and the house sinks into the mirror: real release after twelve minutes of dread. Then the sting reframes it: you freed her from her keeper, not from the house.

**Final sting.** Weeks later, in rain, a different dashboard, fuel lamp blinking. The ROOMS lantern is lit again. A knock; the bell rings off to the right; the door opens by itself. The line that waited for your name reads HARLAN in wet capitals, ruled through, with a blank line under it. From the opening's exact threshold and lens: candlelight, the tally wall, and Ada in his rocking chair with a round, heavy sack tied at the neck in her lap. The rocking stops. She lifts her own head in one hand; with the other she raises the sack by its knot and turns its eyeholes to the doorway, as he once turned hers. Both of them look at you. Smash to black. One bell. THE KEEPING.

## Risks

- Human realism in the closest shots (the tableau, the slats, the dress, the locket look). Mitigations:
- Her face stays under wet hair, except one iris-less eye.
- Harlan's face is never rendered.
- In the locket look, the locket is sharp and she is soft in the depth of field, held 2.5 s or less.
- Backlight, lightning cuts, film grain and stop-motion throughout; every lit shot under 3 s.
- Wet long hair from scratch can look plastic. Use clumped cards with wet specular, drip particles, a spring chain and a low-detail version beyond 6 m, and prototype it in M1 weeks 1-2 alongside the stop-motion A/B test.
- The finale fights ten minutes of conditioning. Mitigations: the ledger and letter make the logic explicit; the locket rule works even mid-chase; the nonstop bell frames the moment; a prompt appears after the first failure; after two failures she waits.
- Players who skip documents may miss the twist. Every document is 60 words or fewer, and the twist also reads without text: the wreck field, your plate on the can, the padlock outside the hatch, the sack, the knifed photos, HARLAN in the guest book.
- The thunder and bell rules may go unnoticed. Each is demonstrated before it is needed (B02 bell, B06 thunder), the storm rhythm is regular, and there are assists and captions.
- Bell decay could soft-lock B08. The 40 s floor resets on death, three thunder rolls fit in 40 s at the B08 storm cadence, and rumbles lengthen after failures.
- Rope-rig readability underpins escape-theorem step 2. Use one mechanism everywhere: the pulley sound in B02, the rope visibly running in C2, the rope zip overhead in the finale, the bolt box clanking when you rattle the door.
- The systemic locket rule adds a branch (finale before fuel). The ending then waits on the can in a quiet house; test this path explicitly in M2.
- Browser performance. Keep one shadow caster in play; bake lightmaps (KTX2); use portal culling one floor at a time; instance the wrecks; pre-warm shaders; offer quality tiers with dynamic resolution. The lighting bake pipeline is the critical path and must exist in week 1.
- Synthesized audio carries the AI's readability. Prototype drip, drip-stop, crack and gurgle in M1, keep the gurgle short and low, and use no synthesized voices.
- AI fairness in a small house. Use the deterministic node graph and room-graph hearing, tune radii per room, never relocate her in view, guarantee the loops, cap hide checks, and add post-death grace.
- The procedural handwriting font blocks every readable. Budget it in M2 week 1.
- Scope cap: 9 spaces plus the car set. If M2 slips, turn the pump and mailbox into silhouettes, cut the wrecks to 3 variants plus fog cards, re-grade the sting car instead of re-trimming it, and cut the look-up pose.
- Moral weight: the player sends a man to his death. His crimes frame it (guest book, cars, your plate, the padlock), and the sting makes the cost register.

## Synthesis notes

**Base:** narrative (THE KEEPING). Tie-break: the judges' overall scores tie 16-16 (KEEPING 9+7, HEADLIGHTS 8+8, BEHELD 7+6). THE KEEPING wins the three criteria the client wrote down. First, its escape logic is deduced from environmental clues. Second, it has one memorable ending with a final sting. Third, it keeps the fixed beats exactly and gives each a second meaning: his bell, his rope, his stopped stroke, his hand turning her face to yours. Its weaknesses are content weight, which can be cut. HEADLIGHTS' weaknesses are structural and cannot be grafted away: a note that dictates the solution, a twist on an optional page, and a back-seat-mirror trope. So the design takes THE KEEPING's story and rules, HEADLIGHTS' production discipline and BEHELD's AI engineering. Synthesizer fixes on top of the judges' lists: (1) The bathroom is cut entirely. Her upstairs anchor is her own nailed door, which she cannot pass ('She can't get past nails'). That motivates the B08 bell lure and keeps the dress out of her reach. (2) One rope mechanism, obeyed everywhere: a counterweight shuts the door, an iron drop-bolt locks it, and only a pull from the parlor lifts it. (3) Finale order: the parlor door stays shut while the bell rings; after the locket look the hall goes silent, the bell stops, she knocks, and Harlan opens a crack to check. (4) Locket rule: a lit, open locket within 4 m in her sight cone outranks your face, so raising it works even mid-chase. You hold it in front of your own face, and depth of field keeps her soft. (5) The bell lure weakens with each pull ('she learns'), with a 40 s floor, reset on death. (6) Harlan's bedroom gets a second door, creating a line-of-sight loop. (7) The under-stair closet opens off the back passage, so it only exists in the finale. (8) The guest-book line waiting for your name reads HARLAN in the sting. (9) 9 walkable spaces + 1 reused car set.
